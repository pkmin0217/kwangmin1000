The Science Behind the Plan: Mifflin-St Jeor Equation

Our recommendations are powered by the Mifflin-St Jeor Equation, widely considered one of the most accurate methods for estimating Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) in clinical settings.

Key Scientific Definitions

  • BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate): The energy expended while at rest in a neutrally temperate environment. It accounts for about 60-75% of your daily calorie burn.
  • TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure): This figure accounts for your daily activity level (exercise, work, movement) plus your BMR.
  • Caloric Deficit/Surplus: We apply a targeted -500 kcal adjustment for weight loss and +300-500 kcal for muscle gain to ensure long-term sustainability.

Expert Health Guides & Unique Insights

Essential Protein Intake Strategy

Protein is the building block of muscle. For fat loss, aim for 1.6g to 2.2g of protein per kg of body weight. Tip: Consuming protein early in the day helps stabilize hunger hormones like ghrelin.

The Power of Hydration Context

Drinking water can boost metabolism by 24–30%. Practical Advice: Drink 500ml of water 30 minutes before meals to naturally reduce calorie intake through improved satiety.

Whole Foods vs. Processed Foods

Focus on whole foods. High-volume, low-calorie vegetables provide essential fiber that feeds your gut microbiome, which is crucial for metabolic health.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How accurate are these calculations compared to wearables?

While the Mifflin-St Jeor formula is a clinical standard, wearables track real-time heart rate. However, research suggests that mathematical formulas often provide a more stable baseline for long-term meal planning than the variable data from consumer sensors.

Can I change my preferences later?

Absolutely! You can re-run the calculator as your weight or fitness goals evolve. We recommend re-calculating every 2-3kg of weight change for optimal accuracy.
